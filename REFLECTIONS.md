## Probabilistic AI

AI systems can produce answers that sound certain even when details are incomplete or out of date. While building this Intel Sustainability Timeline, I treated AI as a tool for brainstorming and explaining code rather than as proof that a fact was correct. Sustainability dates, goals, and figures need to be checked against the company’s own reports and policy pages. I also learned that code generated for one layout can miss important details such as keyboard access, right-to-left reading order, and whether a form is honest about what happens to submitted information. A useful lesson for future projects is to review AI output, verify factual claims with reliable sources, and test the finished experience instead of assuming that plausible output is correct.

## Interview Question

**Question:** How do you balance ambitious environmental goals with the practical challenges of operating a large technology manufacturing business?

I chose this question because the timeline connects long-term commitments with operational work such as conserving water, improving energy efficiency, using renewable electricity, and reducing waste. It invites an interviewee to explain the decisions, trade-offs, and partnerships behind a goal, rather than only repeating a target. If I were interviewing someone who works in sustainability or manufacturing, I would also ask how progress is measured and communicated, and what changes they expect will matter most over the next several years. That follow-up would help distinguish a public ambition from the practical steps used to pursue it.
